A personal journey through everyday ethical decisions.

This portfolio documents real moments from my life this semester when I had to think about what the right thing to do was. For each one, I describe what happened, the values that were in tension, and what the experience taught me about the person I want to be.

Moral Self-Portrait

The beliefs and values that guide how I try to act.

Personal Ethics Manifesto

I am a person who lives by his own principles. I believe in karma and am easily guilted by my own actions and inactions. I value integrity, responsibility, and fairness, and I try to let these values guide the way I treat others and make decisions. I believe that being successful should never come at the expense of honesty, respect, or the well-being of others. When I make mistakes, and I often do, I want to take responsibility for them, learn from them, and become better rather than simply finding someone or something else to blame. As I continue to grow as a student, leader, and person, I want to remain open to learning from my experiences and questioning my own decisions. Ultimately, I want my actions to reflect not only what I am capable of achieving, but also the kind of person I choose to be.

My Three Core Values

  • Integrity

    Doing what is right even when nobody is watching.

  • Responsibility

    Taking ownership of my choices, actions, and their consequences.

  • Fairness

    Treating people fairly while considering different perspectives and circumstances.

The Moral Person I Am Becoming

Ethical Thinking

Looking back at the twelve moral moments in this portfolio, I realized that understanding right and wrong goes beyond simply knowing what is acceptable or unacceptable. Before, I often viewed ethical decisions through the perspective of my personal values and what I believed was right. However, reflecting on these experiences helped me understand that morality also involves considering other people, recognizing the consequences of my actions, and being willing to take responsibility for my decisions. I learned that even ordinary situations, such as helping a friend, admitting a mistake, or choosing not to take an unfair advantage, can reveal a lot about one's character.

Among the different ethical theories I encountered, Aristotle's Virtue Ethics influenced me the most. Its emphasis on developing good character through consistent actions made me realize that being a moral person is not about being perfect or never making mistakes. My experience as our organization's Director for Logistics, when I overlooked reserving a projector for an event, reminded me of this. I made a mistake that inconvenienced other people, but I learned that acknowledging it and taking responsibility were important steps toward becoming a better leader. Similarly, choosing not to use the answers during my certification examination showed me that integrity is something I must practice even when dishonesty could provide an easier path. These experiences taught me that the person I become is shaped not only by major decisions but also by the principles I uphold in everyday situations.

Values

Throughout these experiences, I have also strengthened my appreciation for honesty, responsibility, fairness, and compassion. I learned that fairness does not always mean treating everyone exactly the same, as demonstrated by our decision to recognize the member who contributed most significantly to our thesis as its lead proponent. I also realized that kindness does not have to involve grand gestures. Sometimes, simply approaching a friend who is alone, buying him something, and taking the time to listen can already make a difference. My experience during Undas also reminded me that responsibilities may sometimes conflict, but I should still make an effort to honor the relationships and traditions that matter to me. These moments encouraged me to become more considerate of other people's circumstances instead of focusing solely on my own perspective.

Ethical Growth

As a Computer Engineering student and a future engineer, I know that these lessons will extend beyond my personal life. My future profession will involve making decisions that may affect other people, whether through the systems I develop, the responsibilities I take on, or the people I work with. Technical knowledge and competence are important, but they must be accompanied by honesty, accountability, fairness, and concern for public welfare. I want to become an engineer who does not compromise integrity for convenience, who gives credit where it is deserved, who takes responsibility for mistakes, and who considers the people affected by every decision. I also understand that ethical growth is continuous. There will always be situations that challenge my principles, and I may not always make the best decision immediately. What matters is my willingness to reflect, learn, and improve.

Ultimately, this portfolio has reminded me that becoming a moral person is an ongoing journey rather than a destination. I may not always have the perfect answer to every ethical dilemma, but I can choose to approach each situation with honesty, empathy, humility, and responsibility.

My Personal Commitment

As a moral person, I commit myself to... doing what is right even when it is difficult, treating others with fairness and compassion, taking responsibility for my actions, and continuously striving to become someone whose principles are reflected not only in my words but also in my decisions and actions.
